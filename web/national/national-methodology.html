The new model

The previous estimator used a common municipal transition matrix and constrained expected destination totals to match the observed totals. The new model retains individual electoral sections as observations, shares information through geographic groups and jointly estimates two behavioural regimes. It estimates expected transitions rather than reconstructing individual ballots.

  • Geographic sharing: co-located sections start together. A geodesic nearest-neighbour algorithm merges nearby groups toward 10 sections, subject to a 5-km maximum diameter. These are fixed experimental settings, not validated reliability thresholds.
  • Behavioural sharing: two latent section regimes are inferred together with their transition probabilities. Membership uncertainty enters posterior flow predictions. Similar aggregate changes do not prove identical individual behaviour.
  • Context and residual variation: standardized first-round composition enters the logits. Dirichlet–multinomial moment overdispersion changes the margin covariance. A multivariate normal approximation supplies the observed-margin likelihood.
  • Priors: municipal logits have Normal(0, 2) priors; composition coefficients Normal(0, 0.25); geographic scale HalfNormal(0.5); regime contrasts use scale 0.5, with an ordered reference-logit anchor; mixing log-odds Normal(0, 1); overdispersion Beta(1, 19).
  • Computation: four chains, 700 warmup and 700 retained samples per chain initially. Warning/timeout cases receive one longer attempt with 1,000 warmup and 1,500 retained samples. Both attempts use the same data and remain auditable.

What the intervals mean

Municipal section-flow draws are added before their quantiles are calculated. Intervals are pointwise and conditional on the likelihood approximation, priors, electorate closure, grouping and two-regime model. They are not a probability that individual voters have been identified correctly. Warning fits have provisional intervals. Widths are percentages of the selected origin group; an interval from 20% to 60% is 40 percentage points wide.

The new expected runoff totals can differ from observed totals. The site shows them separately and does not adjust fitted flows afterward. Missing new-model estimates remain unavailable, rather than being filled from the old estimator.

Evidence available before national expansion

The completed pilot used three observed municipalities, 20 paired whole-building resamples per municipality, large-building deletion checks, prior variants and 60 known-flow development datasets based on small-municipality designs. All four model families received identical resampling indices; behavioural groups and geographic partitions were refitted. Resampling ranges describe perturbation sensitivity, separate from posterior intervals.

Errors are mean dataset RMSE per flow cell, normalized by municipal electorate. Comparisons exclude failed/warning fits and are small selected subsets, not nationwide improvement estimates. The combined model passed diagnostics in only 43 of 135 pilot attempts. Its uncertainty was often broad. In one known-flow case all four converged models agreed near 66% retention when the true expected retention was 85.6%.

Model agreement is a robustness measure, not independent evidence of truth. In the pilot, selected agreeing flows still included substantial shared errors. No nationwide bootstrap or prior-sensitivity run is represented as completed. The archived pilot exports and the national manifest document the actual evidence.

How to read the map

Choose a first-round origin, runoff destination and map metric. Grey means unavailable; dashed municipality outlines flag provisional estimates. Computational status reports sampling performance, not model validity. The baseline disagreement layer compares the new estimate with a point-only refit of the original estimator on the same verified electorate. The previous website remains available unchanged in the archive.

Data and reproducibility

Inputs use public TSE two-round totals and audited polling-building coordinates checked against detailed IBGE boundaries. Equal section electorates are required; unresolved locations are excluded and coverage is displayed. Coordinates do not establish residential neighbourhoods or campaign effects.

Academic basis

Puig & Ginebra provide a precedent for joint clustering and voter-transition estimation. Gnaldi, Tomaselli & Forcina explain ecological bias and why multiple methods may share errors. Hennig develops cluster-stability assessment and cautions that stability is not validity. These support research strategies, not a claim that this national release is independently validated.
